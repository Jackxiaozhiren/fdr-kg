\title{Reliable Knowledge Discovery in Knowledge Graphs: A Statistical Quality Assurance Framework for Link Prediction}

\author[1]{Zhiren Xiao\corref{corresponding}}
\cortext[corresponding]{Corresponding author.}
\address[1]{Guangdong University of Finance, Guangzhou, China}%
\ead{241734106@m.gduf.edu.cn}

\begin{abstract}
Knowledge-based systems in safety-critical domains---from drug repurposing to clinical decision support---increasingly depend on knowledge graph (KG) completion to infer missing facts, yet current evaluation protocols provide no mechanism to distinguish reliable knowledge discoveries from statistical guesses. Standard ranking metrics (MRR and Hits@$K$) aggregate thousands of predictions into a single scalar, quantifying only average rank quality without per-prediction reliability guarantees.
We develop a statistical quality assurance framework that reformulates KG completion evaluation through false discovery rate (FDR) control: empirical $p$-values are constructed from embedding scores via permutation-based negative sampling, and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR control, Storey's $q$-value estimation, and Efron's empirical Bayes local FDR are applied in concert to produce per-triple, per-relation, and per-model reliability profiles.
Experiments on WN18RR and FB15k-237 across four model architectures (TransE, RotatE, ComplEx, ConvE) yield three findings directly relevant to knowledge-based system deployment.
First, the estimated null proportion $\hat{\pi}_0$ ranges from $0.259$ (RotatE, WN18RR) to $0.851$ (ComplEx, FB15k-237), revealing that 26--85\% of test triples are statistically indistinguishable from noise depending on the model and benchmark.
Second, per-relation discovery rates span from below $10\%$ to above $95\%$ under a single model, exposing extreme heterogeneity in prediction reliability that aggregate metrics cannot characterize.
Third, the cross-model ranking produced by FDR control differs substantially from the MRR-based ranking: RotatE and TransE each lead on one dimension (MRR vs.\ BH rejections), and ConvE surpasses TransE on MRR ($0.469$ vs.\ $0.560$ on WN18RR) while trailing TransE on statistically significant discoveries at FDR $\leq 0.05$.
ComplEx produces near-zero discoveries on WN18RR and minimal discoveries on FB15k-237 ($1.8\%$ BH rejection at $\alpha = 0.05$), revealing severe score miscalibration.
The framework operates as a model-agnostic post-hoc protocol requiring no retraining and completing in minutes on consumer hardware, providing per-prediction reliability quantification directly relevant to knowledge-based systems that consume KG completion output.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data Availability Statement}
The WN18RR and FB15k-237 datasets used in this study are publicly available benchmark datasets. The complete source code, analysis scripts, trained model checkpoints, and per-relation FDR statistics are openly available at \texttt{https://github.com/Jackxiaozhiren/fdr-kg}. No new data were generated for this study. All experiments were conducted with random seed 42; re-running with the provided code and seed will reproduce the exact results reported in this paper.
